# CLA Faculty Guidance - Fall 2026

This is a public-safe, role-based reference for a faculty member and major advisor
working with both faculty operations and student-facing advising questions. It
summarizes the Fall 2026 College of Liberal Arts faculty memo. It is not a
replacement for current official Cal Poly policy or the current academic calendar.

## Class syllabi

Every instructor must provide clear and timely information about course
expectations in a syllabus. Under Academic Senate Resolution AS-644-06, the
syllabus should be available to each student during the first class meeting and
should include, at minimum:

- Instructor contact information, office hours, and office location
- Required texts and supplementary materials
- Methods and expectations for assessing and grading student performance
- Attendance requirements and make-up policy, when applicable
- Other information needed to explain the nature, requirements, and expectations
  of the course

Make the syllabus available in an accessible electronic form, such as through
Canvas. Address student questions about course expectations promptly.

### Recommended syllabus inclusions

Instructors are strongly encouraged to include clear policies on:

- Academic integrity
- Whether and how AI tools may be used
- Classroom climate and participation expectations
- Attendance
- Campus resources, including resources supporting students with disabilities or
  undocumented status

Use current official Cal Poly syllabus and student-support resources for model
language and links.

## Office hours

Academic Senate Resolution AS-1014-26 establishes weekly scheduled office hours
scaled to the instructional assignment. The minimum schedule is:

| Instructional WTU | Minimum office hours | Credit-hour minutes |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 1-3 WTU | 1 hour | 50 |
| 4-6 WTU | 2 hours | 100 |
| 7-9 WTU | 3 hours | 150 |
| More than 9 WTU | 4 hours | 200 |

Tenure-line faculty with zero instructional WTU who supervise students through
research or other projects must hold at least one regularly scheduled in-person
office hour under college policy.

Scheduled office hours should match student needs for the instructional or
advising function. Acceptable scheduled modes are in-person and synchronous
online formats such as Zoom.

Asynchronous communication and ad hoc appointments are normal instructional
duties, but they do not replace scheduled office hours.

Post office hours in the faculty listing on the department website by the
beginning of the second week of instruction.

## Student schedules and enrollment

Correct enrollment is the student's responsibility. Students should double-check
their schedule in Student Center through the portal; Canvas access is not a
reliable confirmation of current enrollment.

For withdrawals after the drop period, direct students to the current Registrar
Drops, Withdrawals, and Leaves information and the appropriate forms. Registration
procedures change, so verify deadlines against the current Student Planning
Calendar and Registrar pages.

### Late enrollment

The late enrollment petition may allow enrollment after the add deadline during
the late enrollment period. The memo states that the petition requires a fee and
instructor approval. Verify the current fee, dates, form, and approval workflow
on the Student Forms and Registrar websites.

### Withdrawals

The memo describes an undergraduate withdrawal limit of 18 units over the
student's Cal Poly career. Quarter and semester units count one-for-one. Students
may petition to withdraw from a course or the entire term after the drop deadline
and before the final 20% of instruction for a serious and compelling reason,
which can include medical, mental-health, financial, or personal circumstances.

During the final 20% of instruction, withdrawal generally requires serious and
compelling reasons beyond the student's control. Withdrawals determined to be
for such reasons do not count toward the 18-unit limit. The last day to withdraw
is the last day of instruction; no withdrawals are allowed during final-exam
week. Verify the current policy, dates, forms, and college approval route with
the Registrar and CLA Dean's Office.

For CLA majors, withdrawal petitions are reviewed through the CLA Dean's Office.
The current process no longer requires an instructor-of-record comment on
attendance or academic performance under the updated withdrawal policy cited in
the memo (AS-963-23). Use the current official process if an instructor has
concerns about a withdrawal.

## Course modality

Courses must be delivered in the officially scheduled modality: in-person,
hybrid, or virtual. For an in-person course, at least 75% of scheduled contact
hours must be face-to-face. Faculty may shift up to 25% of contact hours to a
virtual format, synchronous or asynchronous, with advance notice to the
department chair or office.

Communicate planned virtual instruction clearly to students and, when possible,
include it in the syllabus at the beginning of the term. Avoid pre-scheduling the
full 25% allowance so that flexibility remains for illness or emergencies.
Consult the current Registrar Course Policies page and applicable Academic Senate
resolution for modality definitions and current requirements.

## Final examinations

Courses with a lecture or seminar component generally require a final examination
unless the catalog description says otherwise. A final may be an exam, paper,
presentation, or another assessment.

- Lab-only, activity-only, and one-unit courses hold the final assessment during
  the last regularly scheduled class meeting.
- Other finals and assessments take place during finals week at the time assigned
  by the Registrar unless the Dean's Office approves a change.
- The assigned time is based on the course meeting pattern and the official Fall
  Final Exam Schedule.
- Students should be told the final date and time as soon as possible and no later
  than the first day of class.

### Changing a scheduled final

Requests to change the scheduled time for an entire class should have a highly
compelling reason, follow applicable regulations, be endorsed by the department
chair, and reach the Dean's Office by the end of 80% of the term. The Fall 2026
memo lists November 13, 2026 as that deadline; verify it against the current
calendar and college instructions.

### Student final-exam conflicts

Under Cal Poly policy, a student with more than two finals on the same day may
ask an instructor to move one final. Faculty should make a reasonable effort to
accommodate the request. The request does not require Dean's Office or department
chair approval. The memo lists May 15, 2026 as the request deadline; because that
date may not match the relevant term, verify the applicable date in the current
official final-exam policy.

### Asynchronous sections

Faculty teaching asynchronous sections are encouraged to use asynchronous final
assessments. This supports students with inflexible work schedules, students in
other time zones, and students who would otherwise face time conflicts.

If a synchronous final is necessary, the memo identifies Saturday before finals
week and evening finals-week blocks as possible options. Verify the current
schedule and approved time blocks. If a student has a conflict between a
synchronous or in-person final and an asynchronous final, the asynchronous-course
instructor must offer an alternate time.

## A+ grading and grade changes

Under AS-996-25, faculty may assign an A+ at their discretion. An instructor who
uses A+ must determine the numerical equivalent. Cal Poly awards 4.0 grade points
for both A and A+ in GPA calculations. Some graduate or professional schools may
recalculate A+ grades differently, so students should check the policy of the
school to which they are applying.

Grade changes are permitted only for clerical or administrative errors, or when
the original grade was I (Incomplete) or RP. Faculty may not accept additional or
missing work after the term ends unless the original grade was I or RP.

Clerical corrections should be made by the seventh week of the following semester
through the online grade-change function in Faculty Center. Verify the current
workflow and deadline in the portal and Registrar guidance.

### Incompletes

Students generally have up to one year to complete work for a course with an
Incomplete. After one year, the I or RP converts to the grade listed as the grade
without further work on the Incomplete contract. If no grade is listed there, the
student receives an F and must re-enroll to earn a grade; senior-project and thesis
courses may be exceptions.

The online grade-change function supports changes for up to four years in the past.
Older changes require the current Adobe Sign grade-change form available through
the portal's Academics area.

## Advising

Professional academic advisors work alongside department faculty advisors to
provide inclusive academic guidance. First-year students should receive advising
support from the Mustang Success Center. Students in their second year and beyond,
including new transfer students, should use CLA Advising.

CLA Advising supports virtual and in-person appointments, generally 45 minutes,
for:

- Registration and academic planning
- General education and other degree requirements
- Referrals to support services
- Major changes
- Academic policies and procedures
- Graduation checks

CLA Advising also offers virtual Zoom drop-ins for quick questions, generally up
to 15 minutes. Current availability, schedules, and contact routes should always
be taken from the official CLA Advising website.

The memo's general schedule is:

- In-person advising: 9 a.m.-12 p.m. and 1-4 p.m., Monday-Thursday
- Virtual advising: 9 a.m.-12 p.m. and 1-4 p.m., Friday
- Virtual drop-ins: 10 a.m.-12 p.m. Monday-Tuesday and 2-4 p.m. Wednesday-Thursday
- Extended drop-in hours during the Fall 2026 drop period and Spring registration
  period; verify current posted adjustments

Any available CLA advisor can help with any question. Use the current official
CLA Advising website, the university advising website, the catalog, and Registrar
guidance for current routing and contact information.

## Key meeting-pattern changes

With the semester calendar, Cal Poly no longer uses the colloquial "Poly time"
pattern in which classes start 10 minutes after or 20 minutes before the hour.
Semester classes start at the top of the hour (:00) or half hour (:30), with a
10-minute passing period when possible. Confirm meeting times in Faculty Center
and the portal.

The University Union hour moved from the historical Thursday 11:10 a.m.-noon
period to Friday 11:00-11:50 a.m. Lecture-component courses should not be
scheduled during that period without university approval. Verify current Academic
Scheduling guidance for semesters.
